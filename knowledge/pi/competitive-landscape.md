# Pi — Competitive Landscape

## Principle
Competition is broader than named vendors. Always evaluate: AI-agent platforms, CPaaS/conversational messaging, customer-engagement/MarTech, incumbent contact-centre/CRM workflows, internal build, and doing nothing.

Never claim Pi is categorically superior. Determine the buyer's decision criteria, then compare evidence against those criteria.

## 1. Conversational / Agentic AI
Examples commonly encountered in enterprise evaluations include Yellow.ai and Jio Haptik.

### Yellow.ai
Public positioning: enterprise agentic AI for customer and employee experiences, human-like conversations across voice/chat/email, multi-model support, agent builder, RAG/knowledge and broad integrations.
Likely buyer reason to shortlist: mature enterprise conversational-AI platform, CX automation, voice/chat, integrations and AI platform breadth.
Pi discovery angle: Is the buyer evaluating a general AI-agent platform or a specific revenue/collections/service/commerce outcome? What actions must the agent complete after conversation? What channels, payment/commercial journeys and India/Middle East requirements matter?
Do not claim Pi wins on integrations, models, accuracy, security or time-to-value without current evidence.

### Jio Haptik
Public positioning: enterprise AI agents for human-like customer experiences, including Voice, Support, Sales and Booking agents; workflow/system orchestration and multi-step actions.
Likely buyer reason to shortlist: conversational-AI heritage, enterprise CX use cases, agent portfolio, workflow integrations and security posture.
Pi discovery angle: Anchor evaluation to the business workflow and completion metric rather than bot capability alone. Explore whether the use case is acquisition/conversion, collections, service or commerce and what system/payment action defines success.

## 2. Customer Engagement / MarTech
Examples include MoEngage, WebEngage, CleverTap and Braze.

### MoEngage
Current public positioning: agentic customer engagement for marketing/product teams, with 1:1 decisioning, AI agents for marketing work, cross-channel marketing, analytics, personalization, transactional alerts and customer data capabilities.
Likely buyer reason to shortlist: existing lifecycle marketing stack, segmentation/data, journey orchestration, personalization, analytics and growing agentic capabilities.
Pi discovery angle: Determine whether the buyer primarily needs campaign/journey orchestration and customer data, or an autonomous agent that conducts two-way conversations and completes a business action. Pi should not be positioned as automatically replacing a CEP; coexistence/integration may be the correct architecture.

### WebEngage / CleverTap / Braze
Treat as customer-engagement competitors when the buyer's requirement centers on lifecycle marketing, journey orchestration, segmentation, personalization and multichannel engagement. Verify current capabilities before making any feature-level comparison.

## 3. CPaaS / Messaging / Communications
Examples in India include Exotel, Gupshup and Route Mobile, alongside global CPaaS providers.

Likely buyer reason to shortlist: channel APIs, telecom/messaging infrastructure, WhatsApp/SMS/voice reach, reliability, pricing and existing vendor relationships.
Pi discovery angle: Ask whether success is defined by delivering communications or by completing an outcome after the communication: qualification, purchase/payment, promise-to-pay, resolution, cart recovery or system update. Do not minimize infrastructure quality; it may be a critical decision criterion.

## 4. Internal build
Buyer rationale: control, proprietary data/workflows, existing AI/engineering capability, avoidance of vendor dependency.
Evaluate: engineering capacity, conversation design, model/runtime operations, channel infrastructure, integrations, observability, governance, security, evaluation, ongoing optimisation and business ownership.
Position buy/build/coexist objectively using buyer requirements.

## 5. Status quo / no decision
Often the strongest competitor. Quantify the cost of current leakage, manual work, delayed response, low recovery, abandonment, acquisition waste or service load. If there is no material cost of inaction, do not manufacture urgency.

## Competitive discovery questions
- What alternatives are being evaluated, including internal build and doing nothing?
- What are the top 3 decision criteria and how are they weighted?
- Is the desired outcome messaging, journey orchestration, conversation automation or completed business action?
- Which channels and systems must participate in the workflow?
- What metric determines the winner?
- What does the incumbent already do well?
- What would make switching unacceptable?
- Who will validate AI quality, security, integration and commercial value?

## Battlecard rule
For every competitor return: why buyer considers them, verified strengths, buyer criteria they may fit, Pi angle to investigate, questions to ask, proof required, traps to avoid, and unknowns. Never create fake weaknesses.
