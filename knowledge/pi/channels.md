# Pi — Omnichannel Commerce & Engagement Playbook

## Seller mental model
Do not sell channels independently unless the buyer explicitly has a channel procurement requirement.

Sell:
Business trigger → audience → best channel/sequence → conversation → action → outcome → learning.

## WhatsApp
Best-fit hypotheses: cart recovery, lead follow-up, payment reminders, purchase/payment CTA, onboarding, reactivation, loyalty, order/service journeys.
Discovery: current WhatsApp provider and flows; delivery/read/reply/conversion; template operations; two-way conversation; handoffs; CTA completion; CRM sync; consent; cost per completed outcome.

## Voice AI
Best-fit hypotheses: high-value/high-intent leads, collections, retention, onboarding, service, surveys and cohorts where synchronous conversation matters.
Discovery: call volume, answer/connectivity, agent cost, conversion/recovery, languages, objection complexity, escalation, compliance, recording/audit requirements.

## Chat AI
Best-fit hypotheses: conversational resolution, qualification, contextual service and continuous interaction where supported.
Discovery: top intents, containment, resolution, escalation, knowledge sources, context continuity and required actions.

## SMS
Strategic role: broad/reliable notification, reminder and fallback channel. Confirm production availability for the account because the June 2026 deck lists SMS under roadmap 'More Channels'.

## RCS
Strategic role: richer messaging/commerce experiences where device/operator reach and buyer use case justify it. Confirm current production availability and market coverage before pitching.

## Email
Strategic role: lifecycle, information-rich and longer-form engagement within coordinated journeys. Confirm current production availability because the June 2026 deck lists Email under roadmap 'More Channels'.

## Channel orchestration questions
- Which channels are used today for this journey?
- What is the cost and conversion by channel?
- Where do customers stop responding?
- Does context survive when the channel changes?
- What determines channel order today?
- Can the current stack branch on conversation disposition/intent?
- What fallback happens after non-delivery/no response/no conversion?
- Can you measure from outreach to completed business outcome?
- Which customer actions can happen inside or immediately after the conversation?
- What consent, frequency, quiet-hour and compliance constraints apply?

## Journey design pattern
1. Define business outcome and KPI.
2. Ingest/identify audience.
3. Segment/cohort using available evidence.
4. Choose first channel based on use case and economics.
5. Capture disposition/intent.
6. Branch, follow up or switch channel.
7. Trigger completion action.
8. Track outcome.
9. Sync to CRM/MarTech/source system.
10. Learn from results and optimize the next journey.

## Metrics
Measure business outcomes before channel vanity metrics. Useful layers:
- delivery/connectivity;
- response/engagement;
- qualified intent;
- CTA completion;
- purchase/payment/recovery/resolution;
- revenue/recovered amount/cost-to-serve;
- incremental lift versus control when a valid experiment exists.

## Guardrail
Never assume 'omnichannel' means every channel is live for every customer. Separate current capability, configured capability, planned capability and buyer-requested capability.
